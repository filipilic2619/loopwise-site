Looking for a Questions for Confluence alternative?

If your team relies on Questions for Confluence and recent problems (questions that don't show up, search that misses answers) have you looking for an alternative, here is what to check before you switch, and how Q&A for Confluence handles it.

What to look for in a Confluence Q&A app

  • Where questions are stored. If the app keeps them in its own storage, Confluence search can't find them and they disappear when you uninstall. Content stored as Confluence pages and comments stays yours.
  • Search inside answers. People search for the problem, which is usually in the answer, not the question title.
  • Permissions. Questions should follow space permissions, with no separate access model to manage.
  • Accepted answers and an Unanswered queue, so experts know what still needs help.
  • Runs on Atlassian, so your data never leaves your Atlassian site.

How Q&A for Confluence works

Q&A for Confluence stores every question as a Confluence page and every answer as a comment:

  • Confluence search finds words inside answers;
  • space permissions and watchers work as usual;
  • accepted answers, voting, tags and an Unanswered queue;
  • a FAQ of solved questions, ordered by moderators;
  • a macro with an Ask button for any page;
  • free for up to 10 users, and it runs entirely on Atlassian.

Moving your questions

  1. Install Q&A for Confluence and open Questions in the space.
  2. Start with your most-viewed questions: ask each one again and paste the accepted answer, so the FAQ is useful on day one.
  3. Link old question pages to their new place, or archive them once the team uses the new Q&A.
  4. Announce it with a short note: "Ask here instead of chat, answers stay searchable."
