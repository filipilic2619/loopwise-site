How to track project costs and budgets in Jira (without Tempo)

Jira knows how many hours your team logged, but not what those hours cost. When someone asks "how much has this project cost so far, and will we stay within budget?", you need hourly rates, a budget and a forecast on top of Jira's time tracking.

What Jira gives you out of the box

  • Worklogs: time logged on each issue, by person and date.
  • Original and remaining estimates: how much work is planned and left.
  • Time tracking reports in some project types, in hours only.

There are no rates, no money and no budgets.

Option 1: Export worklogs to a spreadsheet

Export the issues and their time spent with a JQL search (CSV), add a rate per person in a second sheet, and multiply.

Good for: a one-off answer.

Watch out for: it is manual every time, the issue export gives total time per issue rather than who logged what and when, and rates that change over time make the formulas fragile. The numbers are out of date the moment you save the file.

Option 2: Tempo

Tempo Timesheets with a financial add-on is the classic answer for larger organisations. It is powerful, but it means moving your team to Tempo's timesheets, and paying for several products per user.

Option 3: A cost app on top of Jira's own worklogs

If your team already logs time in Jira, a lightweight cost app can read those worklogs directly. Cost & Budget Tracker for Jira is ours:

  • Hourly rates per person, each with the date it starts, so past work keeps its old price after a raise.
  • Budgets for a project, several projects or any JQL, with an optional period.
  • Spent, forecast and remaining: the forecast adds remaining estimates at each assignee's rate, with On track or Over budget status.
  • A Costs page in every project: cost per person, per month and per epic.
  • Private by default: only Jira admins and the people they choose see rates and amounts.

No Tempo and no new timesheets: it uses the worklogs you already have.

Tips for reliable numbers

  • Ask everyone to log time on the issue they worked on, not on one "general" ticket.
  • Keep remaining estimates current, or the forecast is only as good as the last update.
  • Set rates with start dates instead of editing old rates, so historic costs don't change.
