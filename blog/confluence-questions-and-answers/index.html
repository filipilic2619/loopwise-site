Internal Q&A in Confluence: questions and answers your team can actually find

The same questions get asked in chat every week: "Where is the VPN guide?", "Who approves travel?", "How do I get a test account?". The answers scroll away, and the next person asks again. An internal Q&A in Confluence keeps those answers where people already search.

Option 1: An FAQ page

A single page with headings for each question is quick to start. It works while the list is short and one person maintains it.

It stops working when:

  • many people need to ask, so questions go back to chat;
  • the page grows past a few dozen answers;
  • nobody knows which answers are outdated.

Option 2: Questions as pages with a page template

Create a "Question" page template and ask people to create one page per question, with answers as comments. Everything is searchable, but there is no accepted answer, no voting, and no list of unanswered questions. Someone has to watch for new pages.

Option 3: A Q&A app built on Confluence pages

Some Q&A apps keep questions in their own storage, which Confluence search cannot see. Q&A for Confluence stores every question as a real Confluence page and every answer as a comment, so:

  • Confluence search finds words inside answers, not just question titles;
  • space permissions decide who can see and answer;
  • watchers get normal Confluence notifications;
  • your questions stay in Confluence even if you uninstall the app.

On top of that you get:

  • accepted answers, chosen by the asker or a space moderator;
  • voting and tags;
  • an Unanswered queue for experts;
  • a FAQ of solved questions in the order your moderators choose;
  • a macro that embeds questions with an Ask button on any page.

Getting people to use it

  • Seed it with the ten questions you hear most often, before you announce it.
  • When a question comes up in chat, answer with a link to the Q&A instead of the answer itself.
  • Give each space one or two moderators who check the Unanswered queue weekly.
