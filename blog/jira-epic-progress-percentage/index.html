Epic progress in Jira Cloud: % complete by story points, not just issue count

"How done is this epic?" sounds like a simple question. Jira's built-in progress bar answers it by counting child issues, so an epic with nine tiny tasks done and one huge story left looks 90% finished. Here are your options in Jira Cloud, from free to fully rolled up.

Option 1: The built-in progress bar

Open an epic, or look at it on the project Timeline, and you see a bar split into done, in progress and to do.

Good for: a quick visual check.

Watch out for:

  • It counts issues, not story points or hours, so big and small items weigh the same.
  • It is calculated when you look at it, not stored in a field, so you can't filter, sort or report on it.
  • Sub-task effort is not part of the picture.

Option 2: A "% complete" field filled by Jira Automation

Create a number field such as Epic % complete and a rule that recalculates it:

  1. Trigger: a child issue is transitioned (or a scheduled trigger once a day).
  2. Branch: to the parent epic.
  3. Action: Lookup work items with JQL parent = {{issue.key}}.
  4. Edit work item: set the field to the share of children whose status category is Done. Use story points instead of a count if you want effort, not items. Guard against epics without children so you don't divide by zero.

Good for: having a real field you can show on boards, filters and dashboards.

Watch out for:

  • The value is only as fresh as the last rule run. Bulk edits, moved issues or a paused rule leave old numbers behind.
  • Each run counts toward your automation limit.
  • Smart-value formulas for points and time get long, and sub-tasks need another branch.

Option 3: An epic progress app

An app can roll everything up when you open the epic, so the numbers are always current. Epic Progress for Jira is ours:

  • Story points and issue count: % done by points and by number of issues, side by side.
  • Time: time logged and remaining, including sub-tasks of every child.
  • Warnings: open issues that are overdue, unassigned or not estimated are flagged.
  • All epics of a project on one page: sort by least progress or most work left, with or without finished epics.

It reads Jira with your permissions, changes nothing and stores no issue data. It runs entirely on Atlassian and is free for up to 10 users.

Which one should you use?

  • A quick look at one epic: the built-in bar.
  • A field to filter and report on, and you're fine maintaining rules: the automation field.
  • Progress by story points and time, with sub-tasks, across all epics, always up to date: an app.
